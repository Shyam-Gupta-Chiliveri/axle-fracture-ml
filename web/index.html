How the model was built

Ductility was already measured. Six models learned it from the chemistry, the hardness, and the bending force.

  1. The rows

    385,000 axles. Each row already carries a ductility and a brittleness.

  2. The inputs

    Chemistry, hardness, bending force, the die, and the diameter.

  3. The split

    Seed 42. 308,000 rows train the models, 77,000 score them. Then the inputs are scaled.

    308,000 77,000

  4. The models

    Linear, Ridge, Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and a stack of the tree models.

  5. The score

    The first bar is training, the second is the test. Linear trains at 89.96% and tests at 90.02%. Ridge is the same pair.

  6. Feature engineering

    Five inputs were added. Carbon over manganese, the hardness range, the force range, the alloy total, and hardness times force.

  7. Hyperparameter tuning

    XGBoost and LightGBM were trained again: 500 trees, depth 8, and a learning rate of 0.05.

    The first bar is training, the second is the test. XGBoost's test moves from 89.87% to 89.91%. LightGBM's test moves from 90.01% to 89.98%.

  8. Advanced optimization

    XGBoost, LightGBM, and Random Forest were stacked, and Ridge blended their predictions with 3-fold cross-validation.

    Ductility first, then brittleness. The first bar is training, the second is the test.

    Ductility

    Linear and Ridge test at 90.0235%. XGBoost tests at 89.8683%, and after tuning at 89.91%. LightGBM tests at 90.0093%, and after tuning at 89.98%. The stack tests at 89.9969%.

    Brittleness

    Linear tests at 90.0235%. XGBoost tests at 89.8456%. LightGBM tests at 90.0047%. The stack tests at 89.9968%. Tuning was run for ductility only. Ridge matches Linear, because brittleness is 100 minus ductility.

  9. Training and the test

    Random Forest trains higher than it tests. That is overfitting. Ridge stays level.

  10. Simple and complex

    Green is a test of 90% or more. Linear and Ridge are the simple ones, at complexity 1. The stack, at 9, does not pass them.
